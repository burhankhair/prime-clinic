Back and neck pain

Often caused by long periods of sitting, poor posture, sports injury or whiplash. Treatment combines exercises, joint mobilisation and soft tissue work to ease pain and get you back to normal daily activities.

Spinal stenosis, herniated discs and sciatica

Narrowing of the spinal canal, prolapsed discs and sciatic nerve compression can cause severe pain and neurological symptoms. Physiotherapy helps speed up recovery with a range of treatment techniques.

Spondylolisthesis and spondylosis

A vertebra slipping out of position, and wear and tear (osteoarthritis) of the spine. Both can cause pain, postural change and pins and needles. We rehabilitate you with a combination of treatment methods.

Shoulder dysfunction

Including frozen shoulder, rotator cuff disorders and dislocations. After assessment, we work to reduce your pain and increase your range of movement with treatment supported by recent scientific evidence.

Strains and sprains

Strains affect muscles and sprains affect ligaments, usually after excessive force overstretches or tears the fibres. We manage the acute stage and then put a rehabilitation plan in place.

Bursitis and tendonitis

Inflammation of a bursa or tendon causes pain and swelling at a joint after overuse or trauma. We treat it with specific exercises, soft tissue manipulation and joint mobilisation.

Knee injuries

Ligament injuries, meniscus tears and anterior knee pain. Rehabilitation helps you regain movement, strength, stability and confidence so you can get back to your goals.

Pre and post-surgery

Pre-surgery physiotherapy prepares your body for the operation. Post-surgery physiotherapy restores function by reducing pain and swelling and rebuilding strength and range of movement.

Neurological conditions

Including Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, brain or spinal cord injury, stroke and motor neuron disease. Our neurological physiotherapists focus on your maximum physical potential and independence.

Sport injuries

Fractures, dislocations, haematomas, sprains and strains from training, competition, a poor warm-up or overuse. We assess and diagnose, then plan your return to your previous sporting level.

Fibromyalgia

Physiotherapy helps manage whole-body pain and related symptoms such as headaches, poor sleep and muscle stiffness, with lifestyle advice to keep your pain as low as possible.

Foot and ankle dysfunction

Fractures, ligament sprains, Achilles pain, plantar fasciitis and fat pad syndrome. Treatment is chosen to suit the root cause and can include exercises, balance retraining, joint mobilisation, soft tissue work and sports taping.

Headaches

Headaches can come from stress, lifestyle, head injury or muscle tension around the neck. Treatment can include soft tissue manipulation, joint mobilisation, and relaxing yoga and Pilates.

Hand dysfunction

Hand rehabilitation is a specialist area because hand anatomy is complex. We restore muscle strength, range of movement and fine motor control with exercises, joint mobilisation and soft tissue manipulation.

Vestibular disorders

Inner ear problems such as BPPV or Meniere's disease can cause severe dizziness and loss of balance. We design an individual rehabilitation plan to reduce your symptoms.

Women's health

Incontinence, pelvic floor weakness, prolapse, pelvic pain and separation of the stomach muscles (rectus diastasis). You are seen by one of our female women's health physiotherapists, with core and pelvic floor exercises plus lifestyle advice.

Treatment built around your assessment.

Every plan starts with a detailed history and physical examination, then draws on the treatments below.

Physiotherapy

The centre of our care: a combination of treatments chosen after your initial assessment to restore movement. Treatment is always selected on scientific evidence and shaped around your specific goals.

Joint mobilisation

Controlled, hands-on movement of a joint. Gentle movement eases pain, while firmer movement into resistance eases stiffness and restores range.

Soft tissue manipulation

Therapeutic massage that boosts blood supply, releases muscle knots and speeds healing. Depth is adjusted to your injury and pain levels.

Exercises

Tailor-made programmes to build joint range, strength, balance, coordination, mobility and fitness, set after your assessment.

Rehabilitation and treatment plans

An individual plan from your diagnosis, aiming to return you to your pre-injury state as quickly as possible.

Balance retraining

For people with a history of falls or frequent loss of balance. We find the cause, then use specific exercises and may suggest aids.

Gait retraining

Relearning a normal walking pattern with strengthening, balance work, joint mobilisation, mobility aid practice and visual or auditory cues.

Sports taping

Protects soft tissue and supports joints, for injury prevention and rehab. Used for knee, shoulder and ankle injuries, plantar fasciitis, strains and tendonitis.

Pilates

Controlled exercise that builds core strength with breathing control. Taught one to one and adapted to your fitness level and condition.

Yoga

Balance, stretching and strengthening with a spiritual element, taught one to one by qualified physiotherapy instructors.

Your first visit, step by step.

You do not need a doctor's referral to see a physiotherapist. The exceptions are WorkCover and Department of Veterans' Affairs (DVA) patients, who do.

  1. 1

    Book

    Call 07 3372 8866 or book online, at a time that suits you.

  2. 2

    Assessment

    Your physiotherapist takes your history and examines you to find the cause of your symptoms. Allow 30 to 45 minutes.

  3. 3

    Your plan

    You discuss and agree an individual treatment plan. Treatment often starts the same day.

  4. 4

    Follow-ups

    Later appointments are around 30 minutes, working towards your goals.

What to bring

  • Your doctor's referral, if you have one
  • Your insurance details and a list of your medications
  • Your claim number and insurer details, if you are a WorkCover or third party patient
  • Comfortable, loose-fitting clothing

Paying for your visit

  • Claim health fund rebates on the spot
  • Eftpos available
  • Bulk billing for all EPC patients
  • We invoice DVA and WorkCover directly
  • Insurance accepted, if your cover extends to physiotherapy

Questions, answered.

Something else on your mind? Call 07 3372 8866 and we'll help.

Who are physiotherapists?

Physiotherapists are trained to assess the underlying causes of joint, muscle and nerve injuries and to provide effective treatment. They use drug-free techniques to relieve pain, restore movement, improve mobility and prevent problems coming back.

What happens at my first visit?

Your therapist does an initial assessment and often starts treatment the same day. They will go over your medical history to understand how your condition developed, then discuss a treatment plan with you.

What do I need to bring?

You do not need a doctor's referral. If WorkCover or the Department of Veterans' Affairs is paying, you will need one. Bring any referral you have, your insurance details and a list of your medications. WorkCover and third party patients should bring their claim number and insurer information. Wear comfortable, loose-fitting clothing if possible.

What will the physiotherapist do?

It depends on your diagnosis. Your physiotherapist reviews how your symptoms developed and checks your function, any lost movement and painful positions or movements. This tells us what condition you have so we can design a specific treatment plan.

How long are sessions?

Your first appointment is longer because it includes your full assessment. Follow-up sessions are shorter, at around 30 minutes.

Will you accept my insurance?

Yes, if your insurance cover extends to physiotherapy. We can claim health fund rebates for you on the spot.

Why is physiotherapy a good choice?

Physiotherapy puts you in control of correcting a problem and easing pain, and it can help stop the pain returning. It is safe, drug-free and has no side-effects.
